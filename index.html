Scientific feasibility

Ethanol can leave the wine. Flavor does not have to follow.

Removing alcohol from finished wine is already practiced industrially. The open question for Vinora is whether a peristaltic, dialysis-style membrane loop—gentle pressure, ambient temperature, selectable final ABV—is scientifically sound for both a countertop unit and a winery skid.

Verdict: feasible. Membrane dealcoholization of wine is commercially and academically established. Osmotic distillation (also called evaporative pertraction)—wine on one side of a hydrophobic hollow-fiber membrane, water stripper on the other—matches the dialysis-like architecture you described, and is routinely driven by peristaltic or centrifugal recirculation at near-atmospheric differential pressure.

True dialysis (hydrophilic membranes, liquid–liquid diffusion) also works but tends to strip minerals and phenolics more aggressively than hydrophobic contactors. Reverse osmosis / nanofiltration are pressure-driven (typically 20–35 bar) and scale well industrially, but are a poorer fit for a home device. Pervaporation (organophilic PDMS) is selective for ethanol but needs vacuum on the permeate side.

For dual home + industrial products, osmotic distillation (membrane contactor) is the strongest shared platform: low pressure, ambient temperature, peristaltic loops, and published ethanol fluxes on real wine.

Targets of 0.5–5% ABV are within demonstrated process envelopes. Literature already shows reduction from ~10.5–14.8% ABV down to ~0.5% with multi-cycle strip-water exchange, and partial cuts of 2–4% ABV in a single pass for “lighter” wines. Selectable setpoints are an instrumentation and control problem (inline densitometry / refractive index / NIR), not a membrane physics barrier.

What limits the process is not whether ethanol crosses the membrane—it does—but how much aroma travels with it, how strip water is managed, and how flux falls as the concentration driving force shrinks toward 0.5% ABV.

At ~11–15 °C, hydrophobic PP and PVDF hollow fibers transfer ethanol at roughly 0.11–0.17 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹ during a −3% ABV cut. Organophilic pervaporation at 20–50 °C yields total permeate fluxes of 0.25–1.8 kg·m⁻²·h⁻¹ with separation factors ~6–7. Wine RO permeate fluxes sit near 1.1–1.9 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹; NF is faster at ~4–9 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹ but less selective.

Deep dealcoholization to 0.5% requires refreshing the strip solution (or evaporating/recovering ethanol from it). Expect longer residence times and higher aroma loss than a mild trim to 5%. EU / labeling rules also distinguish “dealcoholized” (<0.5%), “partially dealcoholized,” and low-alcohol categories—product claims must track the selected setpoint.

0.05–0.3 m² hollow-fiber module, dual peristaltic loops, chilled strip water, batch 0.75–5 L. Cycle times of a few hours for a mild cut; overnight for deep 0.5% on a bottle. Consumable: strip water + periodic membrane replacement.

Driving force

In osmotic distillation, ethanol moves as vapor through gas-filled hydrophobic pores because its partial pressure is higher on the wine side than on the strip-water side. No large hydraulic transmembrane pressure is required—only enough ΔP to keep wine from wetting into the strip circuit.

Why peristaltic loops

Peristaltic (or sanitary centrifugal) pumps set independent wine and strip recirculation rates, enable gentle shear for home volumes, and match published lab/pilot OD setups that already use dual peristaltic loops on hollow-fiber contactors.

Limit to 0.5% ABV

As wine ABV falls, driving force shrinks and strip water approaches equilibrium. Multi-cycle strip refresh (or continuous fresh strip / strip regeneration) is mandatory for deep dealcoholization—demonstrated to ~0.5% v/v in OD literature.

Membranes & vendors

Buyable modules, not lab curiosities.

Vinora’s preferred stack starts with hydrophobic hollow-fiber contactors already documented for wine alcohol reduction, then adds organophilic pervaporation where vacuum recovery of ethanol is valuable.

Primary recommendation — osmotic distillation contactors. Microporous polypropylene (PP) X50-class hollow fibers in Liqui-Cel–style Extra-Flow housings are explicitly recommended by the manufacturer for wine alcohol reduction: wine on the shell side, strip water in the lumen, counter-current recirculation, near room temperature and atmospheric pressure.

Upgrade path — PVDF / superhydrophobic HF. Research modules with higher porosity and “lotus-effect” PVDF surfaces have shown ~0.17 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹ ethanol flux vs ~0.11–0.13 for PP at the same −3% ABV cut, with better aroma retention—attractive for premium industrial skids once food-contact supply is locked.

Selective ethanol cut — organophilic PV. Dense PDMS composite membranes (PERVAP™ 4060 family, now supplied by DeltaMem AG / Pervap) preferentially permeate ethanol under vacuum. Ideal as a strip regenerator or as a premium home/pilot path when a vacuum pump is acceptable.

High-pressure industrial alternatives. Thin-film RO/NF (e.g. ACM3, TS80-class) at 25–35 bar remain the workhorses of many winery “alcohol adjustment” skids, often hybridized with OD on the permeate. They are excellent for producers who already run high-pressure filtration; they are a weak fit for a countertop product.

Food-contact & sanitization. Prefer stainless housings where hot-water sanitization to ~85°C is required. PP housings are common for trials but must not be hot-water sanitized. Pre-filter wine and strip to ≤5 µm absolute to limit fouling.

Pervaporation deep dive

When vacuum earns its keep.

Pervaporation (PV) uses a dense organophilic membrane. Ethanol dissolves into PDMS, diffuses, and evaporates into a vacuum permeate that is condensed as an ethanol-rich liquid (~36–46 wt% in wine trials)—no strip-water dilution of the wine matrix.

Sorption

Ethanol preferentially partitions into the organophilic PDMS selective layer versus water (separation factor ~6–7.4 on PERVAP™ 4060 with red wine).

Diffusion

Solution–diffusion through the dense film. Raising feed temperature from 20→50°C lifts total flux from ~0.25→0.77 kg·m⁻²·h⁻¹ (Arrhenius; E_app ~29 kJ·mol⁻¹).

Evaporation

Permeate side held at ~1–10 mbar abs. so permeating species vaporize. Cold trap / condenser recovers liquid ethanol–water for disposal or rectification.

Duty

Best as (i) direct partial dealcoholization or (ii) regenerating OD strip water so industrial plants avoid massive aqueous effluent.

Design implications for Vinora. A home PV appliance needs a quiet vacuum pump, cold trap, and denser safety/controls story—higher BOM than OD. Industrial PV shines for concentrating ethanol out of OD strip water (hybrid OD→PV), cutting wastewater COD and creating a sellable spirit co-product.

Limits. Some aroma esters co-permeate; metals can appear at trace levels in permeate; fouling and support compaction matter at long duty. Pilot on each varietal before locking SKUs.

Hybrid flowsheet (recommended industrial). Stage 1: OD contactor trims wine ABV at 10–21°C with aroma-friendly kinetics. Stage 2: PV or vacuum distillation regenerates strip water and recovers ethanol. Stage 3: optional blend-back of untreated wine to hit the exact commercial setpoint (0.5 / 2 / 5%).

Reference architecture

A dialysis loop, not a still.

Wine loop: peristaltic pump → hollow-fiber lumen (or shell) → return to vessel. Gentle shear, food-grade tubing, optional chill plate at 8–15 °C.

Strip loop: deionized or reverse-osmosis water on the opposite side of the hydrophobic membrane. Ethanol vapor/liquid equilibrium drives transfer across gas-filled pores. When strip ABV rises, dump or regenerate; home units may use single-use strip cartridges.

Control: user selects target 0.5–5% ABV. Firmware estimates remaining ethanol from elapsed permeate/transfer model and confirms with a cheap density or NIR sensor before “done.”

Industrial add-ons: strip distillation to recover ~40–55% ethanol co-product, CIP skid, parallel membrane racks, and aroma-management blending (hold-back of untreated wine).

Go-to-market sketch

Phase 1 — Home: countertop 0.75–1.5 L batch for dinner-party “sessionable” pours (target 5%) and dry-January bottles (0.5%). Sell membranes + strip packs as consumables (razor/blade).

Phase 2 — Prosumer / tasting rooms: 20–100 L day tanks for restaurants and boutique cellars.

Phase 3 — Industrial OEM: license OD modules + control stack to major producers who already buy spinning-cone or RO systems—compete on aroma retention at ambient temperature and lower thermal OPEX.
