Scientific feasibility

Ethanol can leave the wine. Flavor does not have to follow.

Removing alcohol from finished wine is already practiced industrially. The open question for Vinora is whether a peristaltic, dialysis-style membrane loop—gentle pressure, ambient temperature, selectable final ABV—is scientifically sound for both a countertop unit and a winery skid.

Verdict: feasible. Membrane dealcoholization of wine is commercially and academically established. Osmotic distillation (also called evaporative pertraction)—wine on one side of a hydrophobic hollow-fiber membrane, water stripper on the other—matches the dialysis-like architecture you described, and is routinely driven by peristaltic or centrifugal recirculation at near-atmospheric differential pressure.

True dialysis (hydrophilic membranes, liquid–liquid diffusion) also works but tends to strip minerals and phenolics more aggressively than hydrophobic contactors. Reverse osmosis / nanofiltration are pressure-driven (typically 20–35 bar) and scale well industrially, but are a poorer fit for a home device. Pervaporation (organophilic PDMS) is selective for ethanol but needs vacuum on the permeate side.

For dual home + industrial products, osmotic distillation (membrane contactor) is the strongest shared platform: low pressure, ambient temperature, peristaltic loops, and published ethanol fluxes on real wine.

Targets of 0.5–5% ABV are within demonstrated process envelopes. Literature already shows reduction from ~10.5–14.8% ABV down to ~0.5% with multi-cycle strip-water exchange, and partial cuts of 2–4% ABV in a single pass for “lighter” wines. Selectable setpoints are an instrumentation and control problem (inline densitometry / refractive index / NIR), not a membrane physics barrier.

What limits the process is not whether ethanol crosses the membrane—it does—but how much aroma travels with it, how strip water is managed, and how flux falls as the concentration driving force shrinks toward 0.5% ABV.

At ~11–15 °C, hydrophobic PP and PVDF hollow fibers transfer ethanol at roughly 0.11–0.17 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹ during a −3% ABV cut. Organophilic pervaporation at 20–50 °C yields total permeate fluxes of 0.25–1.8 kg·m⁻²·h⁻¹ with separation factors ~6–7. Wine RO permeate fluxes sit near 1.1–1.9 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹; NF is faster at ~4–9 L·m⁻²·h⁻¹ but less selective.

Deep dealcoholization to 0.5% requires refreshing the strip solution (or evaporating/recovering ethanol from it). Expect longer residence times and higher aroma loss than a mild trim to 5%. EU / labeling rules also distinguish “dealcoholized” (<0.5%), “partially dealcoholized,” and low-alcohol categories—product claims must track the selected setpoint.

Feasible

Home device

0.05–0.3 m² hollow-fiber module, dual peristaltic loops, chilled strip water, batch 0.75–5 L. Cycle times of a few hours for a mild cut; overnight for deep 0.5% on a bottle. Consumable: strip water + periodic membrane replacement.

Feasible

Industrial skid

Modular Liqui-Cel–class contactors or custom PVDF bundles, continuous or multi-pass batch, strip-water recovery (distillation / RO) to cut waste and reclaim ethanol co-product. Scales by area, not new physics.

Watch-outs

Quality & ops

Ester losses rise with deeper cuts; cold OD helps. Fouling, wetting of hydrophobic pores, and strip disposal dominate OPEX. Sensory validation per varietal is mandatory before SKU launch.

Reference architecture

A dialysis loop, not a still.

Wine loop: peristaltic pump → hollow-fiber lumen (or shell) → return to vessel. Gentle shear, food-grade tubing, optional chill plate at 8–15 °C.

Strip loop: deionized or reverse-osmosis water on the opposite side of the hydrophobic membrane. Ethanol vapor/liquid equilibrium drives transfer across gas-filled pores. When strip ABV rises, dump or regenerate; home units may use single-use strip cartridges.

Control: user selects target 0.5–5% ABV. Firmware estimates remaining ethanol from elapsed permeate/transfer model and confirms with a cheap density or NIR sensor before “done.”

Industrial add-ons: strip distillation to recover ~40–55% ethanol co-product, CIP skid, parallel membrane racks, and aroma-management blending (hold-back of untreated wine).

Go-to-market sketch

Phase 1 — Home: countertop 0.75–1.5 L batch for dinner-party “sessionable” pours (target 5%) and dry-January bottles (0.5%). Sell membranes + strip packs as consumables (razor/blade).

Phase 2 — Prosumer / tasting rooms: 20–100 L day tanks for restaurants and boutique cellars.

Phase 3 — Industrial OEM: license OD modules + control stack to major producers who already buy spinning-cone or RO systems—compete on aroma retention at ambient temperature and lower thermal OPEX.
